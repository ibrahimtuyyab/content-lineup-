[answer]
Brief an article in four lines: the **topic** in plain language, one **primary keyword**, the **angle** (what this piece says that the existing top results do not), and the **publish date**. The angle is the field that decides whether the draft is worth publishing, and it is the one most people skip.
[/answer]

The complaint about AI drafts is almost always "it came out generic". Nearly every time, the brief was generic first. A model cannot invent a point of view you did not give it — it will produce a competent synthesis of what already exists, which is exactly the thing that cannot outrank what already exists.

[toc]

## The four-line template

Copy this. It is the whole thing.

[table caption="The four-line article brief"]
Field | What goes in it
Topic | One sentence, as you would say it to a writer
Primary keyword | One phrase, the one you actually want to rank for
Angle | What this says that the current top three results do not
Publish date | A real date, decided now rather than later
[/table]

Filling this in takes under a minute once you have the keyword. The discipline is in keeping it to four lines: longer briefs feel more rigorous but usually just restate the topic in three different ways.

## The angle is the whole job

Compare these two briefs for the same article.

[quote cite="A brief that produces a generic draft"]
Write about heat pumps versus furnaces. Target keyword: heat pump vs furnace.
[/quote]

That will produce a correct, readable, entirely unremarkable article — because every page currently ranking for that term already says the same things.

Now the same topic with an angle:

[quote cite="A brief that produces a publishable draft"]
Heat pump vs furnace, but specifically for a dry climate with cheap gas. Every existing guide assumes a mild damp climate and expensive gas, so their running-cost maths is wrong for our customers. Include a running-cost table using local rates.
[/quote]

Same topic, same keyword, completely different article — and the second one has a reason to exist.

## Where the brief lives

Briefs belong in the backlog, not in your head or a document nobody opens. Capture the topic when you think of it, add the keyword and angle when you have them, and promote it into the queue when there is room.

## Brief in batches

Briefing is research-shaped work, so it batches well. You are already in the keyword tool with the whole cluster visible, and the sixth brief costs a fraction of the first. Eight briefs is about forty minutes, which is a month of content decided in one sitting.

[takeaways]
- Four lines: topic, primary keyword, angle, publish date
- The angle is what makes the draft worth publishing — never skip it
- Longer briefs are not better briefs; they mostly restate the topic
- Batch briefing: the sixth brief costs far less than the first
- Capture ideas continuously, promote them into briefs when the queue has room
[/takeaways]
